# Pandemic influence and recursive-ordering sensitivity

These exercises are point estimates using the same harmonised log transformations, two lags and quarterly deterministic controls as the baseline. They are generated by main.m, saved under outputs/tables and shown in two additional figures. They do not provide confidence intervals for differences across specifications or countries.

## Design fixed before reading the results

Four sample treatments are crossed with four recursive orderings in each region (32 fits):

| Sample treatment | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Full sample | 2002Q2–2025Q3 |
| Pre-2020 | 2002Q2–2019Q4 |
| Exclude 2020Q2 | Remove regression equations whose target or either lag contains 2020Q2 |
| Exclude 2020 | Remove regression equations whose target or either lag contains any quarter of 2020 |

Excluding a source quarter is **not** implemented by deleting rows and joining the remaining data. The full calendar and lag matrix are built first, then affected regression equations are removed. With two lags, excluding 2020Q2 removes equations for 2020Q2–2020Q4 (89 effective observations remain). Excluding the whole of 2020 removes equations for 2020Q1–2021Q2 (86 remain). Neither operation estimates a counterfactual economy without the pandemic or its later effects.

| Ordering | Sequence | Assumption changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Oil, GDP, unemployment, inflation, income/GDP, EGM, rate | Original recursive restrictions |
| Money before income | Oil, GDP, unemployment, inflation, EGM, income/GDP, rate | Income and money contemporaneous allocation |
| Rate before money | Oil, GDP, unemployment, inflation, income/GDP, rate, EGM | Money can respond on impact to a rate innovation |
| Inflation last | Oil, GDP, unemployment, income/GDP, EGM, rate, inflation | Prices can respond within the quarter to these innovations, but their within-quarter feedback is restricted |

The last ordering is a stronger alternative timing assumption, not a preferred causal model. All orderings retain oil first and therefore leave that assumption untested. Reordering a given reduced-form VAR changes shock allocation, not its stability roots. The inflation response and EGM innovation are located by their variable identities rather than hardcoded positions.

An additional 188 fits remove each of the 94 source quarters separately in each region, with the same rule for targets and lag exposure. Reporting every quarter helps avoid selecting only influential pandemic dates after seeing the results. Neighbouring exclusions overlap in the equations they remove; their effects must not be added together.

Unstable point estimates are flagged with Stable=0 and missing FEVD shares. Their root and effective sample size are retained. They are not silently removed or interpreted as zero. This differs from the baseline bootstrap, where unstable draws are retained for finite-horizon bands and counted.

## Results at 20 quarters

Baseline ordering, EGM share of quarterly-inflation forecast-error variance:

| Sample | EA | USA |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Full | 1.84% | 6.96% |
| Pre-2020 | 1.54% | 3.73% |
| Exclude 2020Q2 and lag exposure | Unstable | 3.82% |
| Exclude 2020 and lag exposure | 0.44% | 3.32% |

For cumulative inflation, the US share drops from 22.81% in the full sample to 5.83% after excluding 2020 and its lag exposure; the corresponding EA shares are 4.25% and 1.42%. A large part of the headline magnitude is sensitive to the observations used for estimation. This does not identify the pandemic's causal contribution.

On the full sample, the four orderings produce EGM inflation shares of 1.80–2.05% in EA and 6.92–8.45% in the US. The ranking survives these particular ordering checks. However, in the pre-2020 sample, placing the rate before money yields nearly equal shares: about 1.61% EA and 1.63% US.

Single-quarter exclusions produce stable-fit ranges of 0.38–4.34% in EA and 0.95–10.53% in the US. Three EA exclusions (2020Q2, Q3 and Q4) generate unstable point fits; all US exclusions remain stable. The US minimum occurs when excluding 2021Q3 and its lag exposure; the maximum occurs when excluding 2008Q4 and its lag exposure. Thus influence is not confined to the initial lockdown quarter.

The same 2021Q3 exclusion leaves the EA share at 2.59%, reversing the full-sample ranking. The EA root after excluding 2020Q2 is about 1.0191; after excluding 2020Q3 it is about 1.5923. These are failures of point-estimate stability, not zero monetary contributions.

## Interpretation and next step

The full-sample EGM difference is not merely an artefact of one tested recursive ordering. Its magnitude is nevertheless sensitive to pandemic/post-pandemic observations, and some EA fits lose stability. This weakens a claim of a stable structural difference in monetary transmission.

The next useful checks are residual serial correlation and a principled treatment of pandemic volatility, followed by parsimonious shrinkage and stronger identification if causal claims remain the objective. Removing extreme observations is an influence diagnostic here, not the proposed final estimator. The present results do not validate discretionary fiscal shocks, monetary-policy shocks, or a fiscal-to-money mediation mechanism.
